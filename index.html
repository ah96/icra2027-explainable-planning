Scope and Objectives

Robot behavior links task planning (what to do), navigation and path planning (where to go), and motion planning (which feasible trajectories to execute). Explaining a detour, a changed task sequence, or a failed grasp requires evidence from actual goals, maps, planner constraints, and execution. This is particularly important for hybrid and foundation-model-based systems.

This focused workshop brings together explainable task planning, explainable navigation, and explainable motion planning, linked through planner evidence, feasible alternatives, and execution outcomes. Unlike the broad scope of the ICRA 2023 Explainable Robotics workshop, this edition systematically connects planning evidence, executable alternatives, and human use.

Shared navigation and manipulation challenge cases will yield a challenge inventory and an evaluation checklist for explanations of robot plans.

Three guiding questions

  1. Which goals, costs, obstacles, kinematic limits, and safety rules explain a task choice, navigation route, or motion?
  2. Can counterfactual plans, paths, trajectories, and authorized interventions be verified by replanning?
  3. Do grounded explanations improve human understanding and safe correction, and how should faithfulness and feasibility be evaluated?

Call for Contributions

We invite 4-page non-archival short papers (including references) on explainable task planning, robot navigation and path planning, motion planning, their integration, and human-facing explanation. Topics of interest include, but are not limited to:

  • Explanations of task plans, action sequences, and task-sequence changes
  • Explainable navigation and path selection: map evidence, path costs, obstacle avoidance, alternative routes
  • Explainable motion planning: kinematic limits, collision-free and safe trajectories, abandoned grasps
  • Counterfactual plans, paths, and trajectories, and verification of alternatives by replanning
  • Authorized human interventions and verified plan repairs
  • Explanations for hybrid and foundation-model-based planning systems
  • Human-centered communication of planner evidence and user understanding
  • Evaluation of faithfulness, physical feasibility, and explanation quality
  • Shared embodied scenarios, benchmarks, and metrics for explainable planning

Submission and selection

  • Format: up to 4 pages including references, IEEE conference template.
  • Review: two conflict-free reviews per submission; selection by relevance, merit, and discussion value.
  • Presentation: up to 12 posters/demos; four selected papers also give a 5-minute pitch.
  • Demos: laptop-based demonstrations are welcome.
  • Non-archival: no proceedings; author-controlled links are posted only with permission.
  • Submission: link to be announced.

Challenge Cases and Breakouts

Two shared challenge cases span the workshop scope. Participants identify evidence from the operative planner, test alternative task, path, or trajectory choices, and articulate what a human may safely change. The cases enable comparison without mandating one planning architecture.

Case 1: Rerouted mobile robot

A mobile robot changes its navigation route because a corridor is blocked or crowded. The explanation must draw on map evidence, path costs, obstacle avoidance, and alternative routes.

Case 2: Abandoned grasp

A mobile manipulator changes a task sequence or abandons a grasp because a collision-free, safe motion is unavailable. The explanation must connect the task-level decision to geometric infeasibility.

Facilitated breakouts

A. Task plans

Explanations of task plans and action sequences.

B. Navigation and motion

Navigation, path, and motion-planning explanations, including counterfactual routes, trajectories, and verified repairs.

C. Human-centered evaluation

Human-centered communication and evaluation of explanations.

Each group has a facilitator, a rapporteur, advance questions, and a worksheet to specify a reproducible case (goal, robot/planner evidence, user question, alternatives), candidate metrics, and open research priorities. The plenary compares findings and disagreements. Aggregated notes may form an openly shared challenge-and-evaluation summary.
